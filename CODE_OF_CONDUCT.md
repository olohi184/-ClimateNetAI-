# ClimateNetAI Code of Conduct

## Our Commitment

ClimateNetAI is an open research software project committed to fostering a respectful, inclusive, professional, and collaborative environment for researchers, developers, students, practitioners, and other contributors.

We are committed to making participation in the ClimateNetAI community a harassment-free experience for everyone, regardless of age, body size, visible or invisible disability, ethnicity, sex characteristics, gender identity and expression, level of experience, education, socioeconomic status, nationality, personal appearance, race, religion, or sexual identity and orientation.

We expect all participants to contribute to an environment that supports constructive scientific discussion, responsible research, open collaboration, and mutual respect.

## Expected Behaviour

Examples of behaviour that contributes to a positive environment include:

- demonstrating empathy and respect toward other people;
- respecting differing viewpoints, experiences, and technical perspectives;
- giving and receiving constructive feedback professionally;
- acknowledging the contributions of others appropriately;
- respecting academic and research integrity;
- communicating disagreements respectfully;
- accepting responsibility for mistakes and working to correct them;
- supporting reproducibility, transparency, and responsible research practices; and
- focusing on what is best for the ClimateNetAI research community and its users.

## Unacceptable Behaviour

Examples of unacceptable behaviour include:

- harassment, intimidation, or discrimination;
- insulting, derogatory, or inflammatory comments;
- personal or political attacks directed at other participants;
- deliberate disruption of technical or research discussions;
- inappropriate publication of another person's private information;
- plagiarism or deliberate misrepresentation of another person's work;
- knowingly falsifying research results or contribution records;
- intentionally concealing material research limitations or data-quality issues;
- misrepresenting one's contribution to ClimateNetAI;
- claiming unauthorized endorsement by the ClimateNetAI project;
- repeated disregard for reasonable project-maintainer instructions; and
- other conduct that would reasonably be considered inappropriate in a professional or academic environment.

## Research Integrity

Because ClimateNetAI is research software, participants are expected to maintain appropriate standards of research integrity.

Contributors should not knowingly:

- fabricate or falsify research results;
- manipulate validation results to create misleading performance claims;
- conceal significant model limitations;
- misrepresent dataset provenance;
- remove material data-quality warnings without appropriate evidence;
- claim authorship or contribution credit that is not supported by actual contribution; or
- knowingly introduce copyrighted, confidential, restricted, or improperly licensed materials into the repository.

Disagreements concerning research methodology, model performance, interpretation, or scientific conclusions should be addressed through evidence-based and respectful discussion.

## Scope

This Code of Conduct applies within ClimateNetAI project spaces and when an individual is officially representing the ClimateNetAI project in public spaces.

Project spaces may include:

- the GitHub repository;
- issues and pull requests;
- project discussions;
- research-software documentation;
- collaborative development environments;
- project-related research meetings; and
- other official ClimateNetAI communication channels.

## Project Governance and Enforcement

The ClimateNetAI project maintainer is responsible for clarifying and enforcing standards of acceptable behaviour within project-managed spaces.

Reports of inappropriate conduct will be reviewed as fairly and confidentially as reasonably possible.

Depending on the nature and severity of the conduct, appropriate action may include:

- clarification or informal correction;
- a formal warning;
- rejection or reversal of a contribution;
- temporary restriction from project participation; or
- permanent removal from project-managed spaces.

Actions should be proportionate to the nature, context, and severity of the conduct.

## Attribution and Research Disputes

Questions concerning software authorship, contributor attribution, academic authorship, project provenance, or project identity should be addressed using the project's documented governance and contribution procedures.

See:

- `AUTHORS.md`
- `CONTRIBUTING.md`
- `docs/OWNERSHIP_AND_GOVERNANCE.md`

Academic authorship for individual publications is determined separately according to the scholarly contributions made to those publications and applicable academic standards.

## Good-Faith Participation

ClimateNetAI encourages good-faith technical disagreement, scientific criticism, replication attempts, identification of model weaknesses, reporting of negative results, and discussion of research limitations.

Constructive criticism of software, methodology, models, datasets, or research findings is not considered misconduct merely because it challenges an existing approach or result.

Scientific scrutiny and transparent reporting are important to the development of reliable research software.
