# ClimateNetAI Authors and Contributors

ClimateNetAI is an open research software project for climate-aware machine learning and wireless signal prediction.

This file records the authorship, development, maintenance, and acknowledged research contributions associated with the ClimateNetAI software project.

## Founder, Project Originator and Lead Developer

### Olohimai Juliet Michael

**Founder, Project Originator, Lead Developer and Maintainer — ClimateNetAI**

Olohimai Juliet Michael initiated and developed ClimateNetAI as an evolving research software project informed by her ongoing doctoral research in Systems Engineering at the African University of Science and Technology (AUST), Abuja, Nigeria.

Primary contributions to the current ClimateNetAI software include:

- conception and development of the ClimateNetAI research software project;
- research design underlying the climate-aware wireless signal prediction application;
- development and integration of the machine-learning workflow;
- preparation and analysis of atmospheric and wireless signal data;
- implementation of monthly machine-learning models;
- model validation and comparative performance evaluation;
- development of the Streamlit application;
- implementation of RSSI prediction and interpretation functionality;
- integration of model-performance and data-quality reporting;
- research software documentation;
- software release preparation and maintenance; and
- overall project direction and governance.

## Academic Research Context

ClimateNetAI is informed by ongoing doctoral research in Systems Engineering.

The doctoral research has benefited from academic supervision, research mentorship, scholarly review, and institutional research environments.

These academic relationships are acknowledged appropriately in ClimateNetAI documentation and associated scholarly publications.

Academic supervision, mentorship, review, or co-authorship of a publication does not automatically constitute authorship of the ClimateNetAI software. Software authorship and contributor status should reflect actual contributions to the software or associated research artifacts.

## Contributors

Additional contributors may be added to this file as ClimateNetAI develops.

Contributor recognition should reflect the nature of the contribution, which may include:

- source-code development;
- model development or validation;
- dataset preparation or curation;
- testing and software quality assurance;
- documentation;
- reproducibility work;
- research methodology;
- user-interface development;
- responsible or trustworthy AI evaluation; or
- other substantial contributions to the research software.

Where appropriate, contributor roles may be described using recognized research-contribution frameworks such as the CRediT Contributor Roles Taxonomy.

## Academic Publications

Authorship of academic papers associated with ClimateNetAI is determined separately according to the scholarly contributions made to each publication.

A person may therefore be:

- an author of a ClimateNetAI-related publication without being an author of the ClimateNetAI software;
- a contributor to ClimateNetAI without being an author of every associated publication; or
- both a software contributor and an academic co-author where their contributions justify both forms of attribution.

The author list of each scholarly publication should be treated as authoritative for that publication.

## Citation

Users of ClimateNetAI in academic or research work should consult the repository's `CITATION.cff` file for the recommended software citation.

Relevant scholarly publications should also be cited separately when their methods, findings, datasets, or analyses are used.
