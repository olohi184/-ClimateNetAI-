## 1. Project Origin and Ownership

ClimateNetAI was initiated and established by **Olohimai Juliet Michael** as a research software initiative for climate-aware machine learning and wireless signal prediction.

The project has developed from research exploring the relationship between atmospheric variability and wireless communication performance, including machine-learning-based prediction of Received Signal Strength Indicator (RSSI).

ClimateNetAI is an evolving research software platform initiated by Olohimai Juliet Michael and informed by ongoing doctoral research in Systems Engineering. The project is intended to support continued research, software development, collaboration, reproducibility, and research translation in climate-aware machine learning and resilient digital connectivity.

The ClimateNetAI project identity, project direction, official repository, documentation, releases, and associated project materials are maintained under the stewardship of the project founder, subject to any applicable third-party licences and institutional or collaborative agreements.

## 2. Relationship to Academic Research

ClimateNetAI is informed by ongoing doctoral research undertaken by Olohimai Juliet Michael at the African University of Science and Technology (AUST), Abuja, Nigeria.

Academic supervision, research mentorship, peer review, collaboration, or co-authorship of publications relating to ClimateNetAI does not, by itself, constitute ownership of the ClimateNetAI project or its project identity.

Academic publications arising from collaborative research should attribute authorship according to the actual scholarly contributions of the respective authors.

The software project and academic publications associated with the research therefore maintain related but distinct attribution structures.

## 5. Contributors

ClimateNetAI welcomes appropriate research and software contributions.

Contributors retain attribution for their respective contributions subject to the applicable licence and contribution arrangements.

Participation in software development, documentation, testing, research collaboration, academic supervision, or review does not automatically confer ownership of the ClimateNetAI project as a whole.

Substantial third-party contributions should be documented appropriately to maintain transparent attribution and provenance.

## 6. Data, Models and Third-Party Resources

Datasets, trained models, external software packages, APIs, libraries, and other third-party resources used by ClimateNetAI may be governed by licences, permissions, ethical requirements, or usage conditions separate from the MIT License covering ClimateNetAI source code.

Users and contributors are responsible for complying with applicable terms associated with those resources.

The inclusion of a third-party resource within or alongside ClimateNetAI should not be interpreted as transferring ownership of that resource to the ClimateNetAI project.

## 7. Research Integrity and Responsible Use

ClimateNetAI is intended primarily for research, experimentation, education, reproducibility, and the exploration of climate-aware machine-learning approaches to wireless signal prediction.

Predictions and model outputs should be interpreted alongside the reported validation metrics, dataset limitations, methodological assumptions, and data-quality notices.

ClimateNetAI should not be treated as a substitute for operational network measurements or as a guaranteed predictor of telecommunications performance.

## 8. Citation and Academic Attribution

Researchers using ClimateNetAI in scholarly work are encouraged to cite the software using the citation information provided in the repository's `CITATION.cff` file and the associated archived software release where appropriate.

Use of the software and citation of ClimateNetAI do not replace the need to cite relevant academic publications when research findings, methodologies, datasets, or analyses from those publications are used.

## 9. Project Governance

The ClimateNetAI project may evolve to include additional datasets, machine-learning models, environmental variables, wireless technologies, geographic regions, research collaborators, and software capabilities.

Project governance may therefore be expanded over time to address:

- contribution management;
- release management;
- dataset and model governance;
- reproducibility;
- responsible and trustworthy AI practices;
- research collaborations;
- documentation standards; and
- project identity and branding.

Material changes to project governance should be documented transparently in the repository.

## 10. Core Principle

**Open-source software licensing does not eliminate project provenance, authorship, attribution, or identity.**

ClimateNetAI is developed as an open research software initiative while maintaining transparent attribution of its origin, contributors, research foundations, and subsequent development.
